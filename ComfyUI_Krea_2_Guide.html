2

What it does

Two versions, three workflows

Krea 2 makes pictures from text. The official workflows do not edit pictures. What you choose is which version of the model to load, and whether to steer its style.

Turbo: the one you generate withA version of the model taught to finish in 8 steps. Fast, and tuned to look good straight away. Every official ComfyUI workflow uses it. If you are not training anything, this is your file.
Raw: the one you train onThe base model before that speed training. It needs the full number of steps, about 52, so every picture takes several times longer. It exists so people can train LoRAs and fine-tunes on a clean starting point. You can skip the download.
WorkflowYou give itWhat it does with itReach for it when
Text to image A prompt. Nothing else. Invents the whole picture in 8 steps. Always. This is the main event.
Style LoRA A prompt, plus one of nine official style files switched on. Makes the same kind of picture in that file's look: ink wash, stippling, watercolour, retro anime and so on. The template adds the style's trigger words to your prompt for you. You want a consistent, named look across many pictures.
Style reference A prompt, plus one or two pictures whose look you like. Borrows the style of your pictures, not their content: the palette, the brushwork, the mood. The subject still comes from your prompt. You have an example of the look and no LoRA for it. It needs the int8_convrot Turbo file and the style reference LoRA.
3

The parts

What each file you load is for

A workflow loads three model files. Each one has its own loader box on the canvas.

Diffusion model The artist

also called: checkpoint, UNet, DiT  |  field: unet_name
What it does

Makes the picture. This is the model: about 12 billion numbers.

Why you care

It is the biggest file and it works on every step, so its size against your card decides how fast you go. Choosing this file is also how you choose Turbo or Raw.

Text encoder The reader

also called: CLIP  |  field: clip_name
What it does

Reads your prompt and hands the artist numbers that mean the same thing. In the official workflow it does a second job first: it rewrites your prompt (see below).

Why you care

It is a small language and vision model (Qwen3-VL, 4 billion parameters), so it loads quickly. Change only the seed and ComfyUI reuses its last answer.

Set the loader's type to krea2.

VAE The unpacker

field: vae_name
What it does

Turns the artist's compressed result into real pixels.

Why you care

Barely. It is tiny. Krea 2 borrows the one from Qwen Image: qwen_image_vae.safetensors. If you already have that file, you do not need another copy.

The switch that changes your results the most

Prompt enhancement ("Refine Prompt")Before anything is drawn, the workflow asks the reader to rewrite your prompt into a much longer, more detailed one, and that is what gets drawn. It is on by default. On, a five-word prompt still gives a rich picture, but the rewriter decides everything you did not say: the lighting, the angle, the background. Off, the model draws exactly your words, so short prompts come out plain and long ones come out the way you wrote them. If a result surprises you, this switch is the first thing to check.

Add-ons and words you will keep seeing

LoRA

A small patch file that sits on top of the artist and bends it toward something: a style, a face, a subject. Strength is how hard it bends. 0 is off, 1.0 is what its maker intended, and well past that the picture overcooks. It has to be made for Krea 2.

Trigger words

A phrase a LoRA was trained to respond to. Without it in the prompt, the LoRA may do little. The official template looks up the phrase for the style you pick and adds it for you. In other workflows you type it yourself.

Checkpoint

Just means a saved model file. People use it loosely for the diffusion model.

.safetensors

The container, like .zip or .mp4. It tells you nothing about what is inside. The name is literal: the file can only hold numbers, so it cannot hide a program the way the older .ckpt and .pt files could.

File names

Which one to download

Read a model's filename left to right and it tells you exactly what you are getting.

krea2_turbo_fp8_scaled.safetensors

turboWhich version. turbo is the 8-step one you generate with. raw is the base for training.
fp8_scaledHow its numbers are stored. This is the part that sets the file size.
.safetensorsThe container. Ignore it.

The one idea behind all the letters

A model is a giant list of numbers. The letters say how many bits each number is stored with. Fewer bits: smaller file, more of it fits on your card, slightly less exact result. Same trade as JPEG quality or MP3 bitrate.

bf16

16 bits per number. The original, full-quality file, and the biggest one.

fp8_scaled

8 bits per number, with a scale factor kept for each layer so less is lost in the rounding. Half the size of the 16-bit original. It is the long-standing 8-bit format and runs on nearly any setup.

int8

8 bits per number, stored as whole numbers. About half the size of the 16-bit original.

convrot

The trick that makes int8 hold up. Before rounding, the numbers get shuffled so a few extreme values cannot wreck the precision of the rest. You set nothing. Read it as "the good int8". It wants an up-to-date ComfyUI.

mxfp8

An 8-bit format that keeps a scale for every small block of numbers. Like nvfp4, it is made for RTX 50 cards with a recent PyTorch.

nvfp4

NVIDIA's 4-bit format. Less than a third the size of the original. It is made for RTX 50 cards, which can do the math on it directly.

The knobs on the artist

Turbo has very few knobs worth touching, which is the point of it. Change one at a time, with the seed fixed.

The picture gets sharper and holds more small detail. Time and memory climb at least as fast as the pixel count: 2K has four times the pixels of 1K. Your hardware

Why it is slow, and what the errors mean

On a 16 GB card this one fits. The artist is 12.2 GB, so it can sit on the card whole.

VRAM The workbench

The graphics card's own memory. Whatever is sitting on it gets worked on at full speed, so this is the number that decides what is comfortable. This guide assumes 16 GB.

RAM The shelf

The PC's regular memory. Model files wait here when it is not their turn, and anything that does not fit on the workbench lives here. This guide assumes 32 GB.

Offloading The shuffle

When a model is bigger than the workbench, ComfyUI keeps the overflow on the shelf and swaps pieces on and off the card as each is needed. It is automatic. The result comes out the same. It only costs time.

The numbersThe default set is about 17 GB of model in total: artist 12.2, reader 4.9, unpacker 0.2. They do not all sit on the card at once. They take turns: the reader goes first and steps aside, the artist does its 8 steps, the unpacker finishes. On 16 GB nothing has to spill at 1K. On 12 GB the artist is right at the edge, so a little of it spills and it runs slower. On 8 GB most of it spills. It still works, just slowly, and that is the case the 7.2 GB nvfp4 file was made for if your card is an RTX 50.

Too slow, or out of memory? Pull these in order

  1. Lower the megapixels. Back to 1. The biggest saving for one click.
  2. Turn Refine Prompt off. The rewrite is extra waiting before the first step.
  3. Check the steps. Turbo should say 8. If it says 20 or 50, that is your time.
  4. Smaller files. On an RTX 50 card, the nvfp4 Turbo file.
  5. Free the machine. Close everything else that uses RAM or the graphics card.

What an error is telling you

Red boxes when a workflow opens
Missing custom nodes, or the workflow needs a newer ComfyUI. Update first, then let ComfyUI Manager install what is missing.
"Value not in list" on a loader
That file is not in the right models folder, or it has a different name than the workflow expects. Pick yours from the dropdown.
"CUDA out of memory" / "Allocation on device"
The card ran out of VRAM. Work down the list on the left.
Dies after the last step finished
Usually the VAE running out while unpacking. Lower the resolution.
The whole PC crawls or freezes
RAM is full and Windows is using the disk as memory. Close other programs, or move to smaller files.

The other words in this corner

CUDA / PyTorch The plumbing

PyTorch is the math engine ComfyUI is built on. CUDA is NVIDIA's layer that lets PyTorch use the graphics card. Both come bundled with ComfyUI and you never touch them. You only meet them in messages: "CUDA out of memory" means the card is full, and a tag like cu130 in the startup log is just the CUDA version PyTorch was built for.

Custom node An add-on

Every box on the canvas is a node. Custom nodes are boxes written by other people, installed through ComfyUI Manager. Krea 2 itself needs none: its nodes are built into ComfyUI. You only need a pack when a workflow you downloaded uses one. Each pack is code running on your PC, so stick to well-known ones.

rgthree A popular pack

A set of convenience nodes that shows up in a lot of shared workflows. The two you will meet most: a LoRA loader that stacks several LoRAs in one box, and switches that turn whole groups of a workflow on and off.

First run

A known-good starting point

Get one plain picture working with the official setup before you touch anything clever.

The recipe

  1. Update ComfyUI. Older builds do not know the model.
  2. Open the built-in template. Workflow menu, Browse Templates, search for Krea 2, pick the Turbo text to image one.
  3. Get the files. ComfyUI lists what is missing and where it goes. It is the first three from section 4.
  4. Leave the defaults. 1 megapixel, 8 steps, CFG 1, euler, simple.
  5. Run it. The first run is the slowest, because every file has to come off the disk.
  6. Run it again with Refine Prompt off. Same seed. Now you know what the rewriter was adding.

The prompt

With Refine Prompt on, your prompt is a brief. Say the things you care about and let the rewriter fill in the rest. Anything you leave out, it will decide for you.

With it off, you are the rewriter. Long and specific beats short and vague: subject, setting, light, lens, mood, style.

Words in the picture: put the exact text in quotes, such as a book titled "Field Notes".

Style LoRAs: pick the style in the template and it adds the trigger words. Anywhere else, type them into the prompt yourself.
